## 2. Surviving findings, ranked by whether they can overturn a row

### R1 (blocker, overturns channel 19): the lowest-epoch bound is not taken over the fourteen epochs the rule states

Raised by four refuters (floor-and-control, credits, consistency, framing); recomputed here and confirmed.

The rule (amendment 6, restated in the record section and appB): A_low is "the minimum over all fourteen epochs" of the range's median net of the floor, and "a channel whose A_low passes stays undetermined". The code (`rd = [x for x in rd if x and x["measured"]]; if rd: vals.append(...)`) appends an epoch only when at least one class of the range is measured above the floor, so an epoch at the floor is dropped from the minimum instead of being the minimum.

On channel 19's short BAO range the cadence epoch 20260917161408 reads +0.19 and -0.61 scatters on the two polarisations of the 9.8 m class and +1.20 and +0.22 on the 19.5 m class: no class above the three-scatter gate. By the rule as written its lowest epoch is at the floor and the bound cannot excise, exactly as the text says of 20 and 34 ("on 20 and 34 the lowest epoch is not measured above the floor on either BAO range ... so the bound cannot excise them"). The code skips that epoch and takes D3 (+3.94 scatters, 2.33e-4 net) as the minimum, giving the 15.7 dB row. The other thirteen epochs are measured on the 9.8 m class (+2.82 to +6.91 scatters). The text's "2.8e-4 net of the floor" for 19's lowest epoch is the keep-all four-dump median (bao_short_A_net 2.778e-4); the value the 15.7 dB bar uses is 2.33e-4 (16.5 dB at 2.78e-4, 15.7 dB at 2.33e-4, as the CSV's R_deployed 44.29 confirms). The consistency refuter adds that the code disagrees with itself on channel 20: 20's short range is stopped at the keep-all reading before the bound branch runs, while the minimum over its measured epochs (2.85e-4 at 16:06:08, attributed) would have excised it under the same branch. Channels 30 and 31 are measured on every class at all fourteen epochs (three refuters agree; not rechecked here), so they are unaffected.

What answers it: an analysis on data in hand, either way. (a) Honour the written rule: an epoch with no class measured above the floor makes the bound "at floor" for that range; 19 becomes undetermined at the floor on the short range (its long range is already at the floor), the excised set drops to twelve, the short-only set to seven, the undetermined set to ten, and the abstract, record section and appB counts and lists change. (b) Or write a numbered amendment restricting A_low to epochs with a floor of matching frame count (the four science dumps, all measured on 19's 9.8 m class, min D3), with the reason, and state in the record section that one four-frame cadence epoch reads at the floor there. Under either, correct 2.8e-4 to 2.3e-4, and make the reason string name the epochs the minimum ran over.

### R2 (blocker on channels 23 and 27; major on 19, 25, 26; a sentence on 31): the persistence fallback prices the credited remainder at the coherence of the share the credit removed

Raised by four refuters (floor-and-control, ranges, credits, framing), each from a different direction; the probe arithmetic was recomputed here.

The mechanism. The second addendum credits the ground filter with surviving power 1 - rho^2, rho the between-epoch phasor coherence over 5 to 22 h: by construction it removes the share of the residual that is constant across the day. The first addendum then takes, where the level estimator returned no measured class, the 0.3 m persistence bound (tau >= 3600 s, G 85,831) "only where the between-epoch phasor coherence of the range's classes ... exceeds 0.7 in median, which is a measurement of persistence by a different estimator". But rho > 0.7 is evidence that the removed share dominates; it says nothing about the coherence time of what is left, which is what G multiplies. The level estimator (a structure function, blind to the constant share) is the instrument that reads the remainder's timescale, and on these ranges it returned either "constant" (plateau not positive at two standard errors: no fluctuating share detected at any lag) or "refused" with finite probes. In both cases the remainder's tau_c is unmeasured between the within-dump gain and the bound, which is the case the first addendum itself labels "unpriced" when rho <= 0.7. The 0.7 threshold therefore decides five of the eight short-only rows.

The numbers (cadence_tau_ns32/ns64.csv probe columns; bars from the CSV's R_deployed scaled by G):

- 27: 9.8 m probes 174/208/368 s (refused, spread 2.1); 19.5 m constant/21/25 s. Bar at the three 9.8 m probes: -2.5, -1.7, +0.8 dB against the table's +10.7. Its long range is measured at 31 s and reads 3.9 dB under tolerance at the floor.
- 23: 9.8 m probes 52/93/163 s (refused, spread 3.1); 19.5 m constant. Bar -3.0, -0.5, +1.9 dB against +15.3.
- 25: 9.8 m constant/constant/315 s; 19.5 m 229/487/488 s. Bar 6.5 to 9.8 dB at the finite probes against +18.5.
- 26: 9.8 m 195/500/509 s; 19.5 m 291/871/878 s. Bar 4.8 to 11.4 dB against +17.5.
- 19: 9.8 m constant; 19.5 m constant/283/296 s. Bar about 4.7 dB at 283 s on its lowest-epoch amplitude against +15.7 (moot if R1 is applied).
- 31 (long range): persistence bound, 28.0 dB; the bar stays above the 3 dB allowance for any G >= 237, i.e. any remainder tau_c above about 10 s, and its short range is independently measured at 2521 s (25.4 dB). The excision is robust and should be stated so rather than resting on the fallback.

The campaign's own measured cases contradict the transfer: on the channels bound at 3600 s on the 0.3 m class whose long range is measured, the text itself says "the long BAO range decorrelates within tens of seconds to 48 minutes". The text's tnote "level estimator refused on every class" is also wrong in letter on 19 (9.8 m: constant), 23 (19.5 m: constant) and 29 (9.8 m: constant), which the code's persistence branch emits regardless of status. The first audit's S5 (refused tau booked at the pessimistic end on the exclusion side) has not dissolved; it has moved to the short range on 19, 23, 25, 26, 27.

What answers it: an analysis on data in hand plus a rule statement. Either (a) rule these ranges "measured above the floor but unpriced" (undetermined), as the addendum already does when rho <= 0.7, leaving 15 and 22 as the short-only excisions with a measured coherence time and 16 as the marginal one; or (b) report the bar at the least finite probe and at the persistence bound and excise only where both clear the allowance (23 and 27 become undetermined; 25 and 26 keep 4.8 and 6.5 dB; 19 is governed by R1); or (c) state the excision as conditional, "excised if the non-persistent share holds for more than N s" with N per channel (about 612 s on 27, 212 on 23, 101 on 25, 128 on 26). In every case print the three probe values on each refused row, write "constant or refused" with the true status, say for 31 that the bar survives any remainder tau_c above 10 s, and add one sentence acknowledging that the credit and the persistence gate read the same rho in opposite directions and why the author considers that admissible if the fallback is kept.

### R3 (blocker on the framing of the eight short-only rows): the forecast of record has no baselines shorter than 20 m

Raised by one refuter (ranges-and-gains); verified here. The bank's meta reads experiment "CHIME (RadioFisher 'yCHIME', mode icyl)", expt_overrides {}; RadioFisher's CHIME dictionary sets Dmin = 20 m and Dmax = 128 m; RFIsher's survey.py defaults n(x) to the synthetic table nx_CHIME_800_synth.dat, whose docstring in layout.py says "cut baselines with d <= Dcut = Ddish = 20 m ... the tables written here have zero density below 20 m", and whose first non-zero row is at x = 0.0665, d = 19.9 m at 800 MHz. The refuter's further statement that under the noise-shaped template the residual is defined only where n(u) > 0 was not checked in the RFIsher code, but it follows from the template's construction (residual power proportional to the noise power, which is infinite where the density is zero, in modes that carry no Fisher weight).

Consequence: lambda in both worlds is the bias tolerance of a Fisher matrix in which the 0.3, 2.4, 9.8 and 19.5 m classes do not exist. The "short BAO baselines (9.8 and 19.5 m)" range lies wholly below the forecast's own cut, so the eight rows "excised on the short BAO baselines only" hold bins to a tolerance the forecast never priced on those baselines, and the abstract's description of the ranges as those "which carry the 21 cm BAO scales" is supported by the forecast only for the long range (22 to 78 m, inside Dmin to Dmax). The ranges paragraph's "k of 0.03 to 0.3 per Mpc lie at baselines of roughly 10 to 100 m" is a k_perp heuristic; the forecast's domain starts at 20 m. Nothing in ch09 states the cut. The five long-range excisions are unaffected.

What answers it: a decision, then either an analysis or a relabelling. State in the record section that the forecast of record is the RadioFisher CHIME layout with Dmin = 20 m, Dmax = 128 m and a synthetic n(u) with zero density below 20 m. Then either (a) rebuild the bank with the as-built density (layout.py, dcut = 0) and re-derive lambda, in which case the short classes enter the priced domain, but so do the 0.3 and 2.4 m classes and their 5.7 to 40 dB readings, so the direction is not toward keep; or (b) rename the 9.8 and 19.5 m range "below the forecast's baseline cut", carry the eight as "measured above the floor on baselines the forecast does not price", and count five excisions in the abstract, ch01, ch07 and ch11. Option (a) is the substantive one, because the as-built array has these baselines and the survey uses them; it is a bank rebuild, not a sentence.

### R6 (major): the floor and the gate, and what the eight short-only detections are

Merged from the floor-and-control refuter's three findings (floor character, channel dependence, gate statistic); the zero counts were recomputed here.

(a) Character. The control's excess is a genuine persistent term at 0.3 and 2.4 m (no zero bins). From 9.8 m outward it is largely rectified noise: the estimator clips at zero, and 5 to 30 of the 52 control values per class and polarisation read exactly zero (19.5 m pol 1: 14; 39 m pol 0: 19; 78 m pol 0: 30; 44 m pol 1: 13; 66 m pol 1: 19; 24 m: 21 and 15), so a zero-mean quantity returns a positive mean by construction. "A positive, persistent excess on every class and epoch (zero on one polarisation of the 78 m class)" overstates the first and understates the second; the abstract's "persistent excess on every baseline class" likewise. No attribution is offered, as S10 asked: the refuter's candidates (adjacent-channel splatter from 36, excluded by 36 reading at the floor on the BAO ranges; band-71 downlink above 617 MHz, outside the recorded band; instrumental spectral structure on a near-autocorrelation baseline, favoured by the 0.3 m polarisation asymmetry) belong in the text. Channel 37's zero point (10th percentile within 40 MHz) is one-sided at the band edge, its window containing 31, 33 and 35. The control also carries a diurnal term the pooled floor averages (attributed: 44 m pol 0 reads 6.5e-4 on the daytime pilot dump against 0 to 9e-5 on D1 to D3).

(b) Channel dependence. The floor is assumed the same on every channel and no bound is given. The eight short-only rows clear the gate by 0.1 to 1.7 scatters (15 +3.1/+3.2, 16 +3.1/+3.5, 23 +3.3, 25 +3.6, 22 +4.1, 26 +4.2, 19 +4.7, 27 +4.7) against +17 or more on the five long-range rows. The only in-data bound on the floor's channel dependence is the spread of the channels that read at the floor on the same classes (32 +1.2/+1.3, 34 +1.8/+1.0, 20 +0.1/+1.3, 36 +0.4/-0.3, 29 +0.3/+0.5; 21 +0.5/+2.4 and 28 +2.5/+2.4 sit above them). A floor set at the 34 level would leave none of the eight measured. The record's "at 9.8 m it is 9 to 17 times the floor mean" is the ratio to a mean that is itself 0.3 scatters, not the gate margin. The 9.8 m profile across channels (20 and 21 at the floor between 19 and 22) is channel-specific rather than a smooth trend, which supports a real per-channel excess; that should be shown.

(c) Gate statistic. The gate compares a median over 13 bins and four dumps with three per-bin scatters, so it is about 6 to 13 sigma of the statistic actually compared (attributed bootstrap); consequences: noise cannot flip 16 or 27 (in the author's favour; say so), and "not measured above the floor" is not "consistent with the floor" (21 and 28 read well above the control and are booked at the floor).

What answers it: rewrite the floor paragraph in two parts (a measured persistent term on the shortest classes with candidate attributions and the evidence against each; a rectified-noise floor at 9.8 m and beyond with the zero fraction per class); print per row the gate margin in scatters, the classes and polarisation measured, and the null-population spread as the floor's systematic uncertainty; rank the excisions by margin and say plainly that the eight clear the gate by under two scatters and the five by seventeen or more; state the one-sided reference window; change the abstract to "a positive floor on every baseline class, persistent on the shortest"; add a control block at both band ends to the post-upgrade dump, not only outside the allocation.

### R7 (major): the ground-filter credit on the eight is a stipulation, not a measurement

Merged from the credits refuter (0.95 clip; 1 - rho^2 form) and the ranges refuter (polarisation averaging); rho and rho_noise verified from lag_coherence_tenclasses_D.csv.

(a) Polarisation. cadence_lags_tenclasses.py reads "both pols averaged" (docstring and line 27), so the rho that sets the credit is polarisation-averaged while A is read on the larger polarisation, the very averaging amendment 5 rejected for the level because "instrumental phases oppose". Averaging opposing phasors lowers rho, lowers the credit and raises every bar, the direction against the channel. The text never states the basis; the first audit's S10 asked for it.

(b) Readability and the clip. On the eight's ruling classes rho_noise is 0.2 to 0.6 (15: 0.32/0.27; 16: 0.35/0.28; 19: 0.30/0.62; 22: 0.24/0.30; 23: 0.52/0.41; 25: 0.29/0.31; 26: 0.21/0.41; 27: 0.20/0.46), against 0.01 to 0.08 on the five. The rule's answer to an unreadable rho is the ceiling test at 0.95 (10.1 dB), but 0.95 has no stated basis in the predeclaration, the notes or the chapter, and the same residual reads 0.91 to 1.00 on its 0.3 m class. By my arithmetic at rho = 0.99 (17.0 dB; the refuter labelled this 0.98, which is 14.0 dB) 16 and 27 flip, 19 and 22 fall inside the allowance (2.8 and 0.4 dB) and 15 sits at 3.3 dB; at 0.98, 16 flips and 27 and 22 are marginal. The clip converts "fully removable" into "at most 10.1 dB removable" in the excising direction. It is inert on the five (rho 0.66 to 0.84, resolved).

(c) Form and premise. rho is Re(m_a conj m_b)/(|m_a||m_b|), a phase coherence of mean phasors; 1 - rho^2 is the surviving power fraction only for a constant plus small isotropic fluctuation. Amplitude modulation at fixed phase leaves rho near 1 (credit overstated, channel-favourable, harmless for exclusions); at rho <= 0.5 the form is not a power fraction of anything. The credit's premise is multi-year stationarity at fixed LST, which six pairs within one day cannot measure. None of this is stated.

What answers it: run cadence_lags per polarisation and credit from the polarisation of A; either justify the ceiling from a measurement (the noise-corrected rho on the same channel's 0.3 m class, with the argument that longer baselines cannot be more coherent than the shortest) or treat a range whose rho is not readable above rho_noise as "credit unmeasured" and the channel unpriced there, in parallel with unmeasured tau_c; print rho_noise and the flip rho per excised row; state the derivation of 1 - rho^2, its two failure modes, and that the credit is the within-day-constant share under RFIsher's per-day model, neither an upper nor a lower bound on the multi-year filter.

### R8 (major): the text does not describe what the code does on range aggregation, and the choice is sometimes against the channel

Merged from four refuters; verified from ruling_baseline.py.

- A_net is the median over the classes measured above the floor (`median([x["A_net"] for x in measured])`); the text says "the median over the range's classes". On the six one-class rows the difference is 0.6 to 2.9 dB (attributed: 19 -2.9, 27 -2.2, 26 -1.8, 25 -0.8, 22 -0.7, 23 -0.6), no flip.
- tau and rho are taken over all classes of the range (`for c in RANGES[rng]`), including classes where the channel's excess is at the floor, so on the six one-class rows the 19.5 m class contributes the floor's tau and rho, not the channel's (the control itself reads rho 0.48/0.46 there with noise above 0.8). Reading rho from the excess-measured class alone lowers the bars (attributed: 27 to 6.6 dB, 19 to 10.4, 26 to 13.1, 23 to 16.3, 25 to 20.5), the channel-favourable direction the rule requires. Channel 18's short-range "keep" takes its 15 s from the 19.5 m class where its excess is 1.2 scatters, while its excess-bearing 9.8 m class refused (15/112/17 s; +4.1 dB at 112 s), so that range is unpriced rather than keep.
- The two-class aggregate is a geometric mean (`gmed`), in the chapter but not in amendment 8, which says "median"; S2.2 asked for the least value or a predeclared choice. On 35's long range 1932 and 76 s give 383 s (17.0 dB) where the least value gives 10.0 dB; on 17, 38 against 20 s is 2.7 dB. No flip, but 7 dB of 35's bar rests on an undeclared aggregation rule.
- Bounds: `bnd[0]` takes the first bound class in range order, not the lower; a bound is ignored whenever any class is measured; the persistence branch returns the (0,1) value whatever its status, so 20, 28 and 34 carry "persistence" at 86,164 s where the text says "refused, cap".
- RANGES["shortest"] is (0,1) alone; the 2.4 m class is in no range, while the abstract, record and appB define the range as 0.3 to 2.4 m.

The positive statement the campaign supports and the text omits: all five long-range excisions survive at every probe value on every class of the range (attributed and consistent with the CSV: 17 >= 12.5 dB, 30 >= 16.1, 31 >= 4.2, 33 >= 4.3, 35 >= 6.3).

What answers it: reword the record section and amendment 8 to the code's rule where the code is right (median over excess-measured classes; shortest = 0.3 m; bounds ignored when a measured value exists), and change the code where the text is right (tau and rho from the excess-measured classes; minimum over bound classes; "refused (cap)" on a refused (0,1)); log the geometric mean as a numbered amendment with its timestamp; print per-class tau, rho and bar on every row; add the sentence on the five at every probe; report 18's short range as unpriced.

### R9 (major): coherence times between 1200 and 3600 s are interpolations across an empty lag gap

## 3. Verdict

The five long-range excisions are secure. 17 (16.5 dB, measured on both polarisations and every class, survives every probe at 12.5 dB or more), 30 (28.6 dB on a lowest epoch measured at all fourteen epochs), 31 (28.0 dB, robust to any remainder coherence time above 10 s and independently 25.4 dB on its measured short range), 33 (25.8 dB, 4.3 dB at the least probe on a refused class; the row awaits the rescan) and 35 (17.0 dB; 10.0 dB at the least measured class, 6.3 dB at the least probe; the thinnest of the five, resting on an undeclared geometric mean) are measured 17 to 320 scatters above a floor that is itself rectified noise on those classes, on resolved credits (rho_noise 0.01 to 0.08), and inside the forecast's baseline domain. What they need is wording: the probe-robustness sentence (R8), the geometric mean logged as an amendment, 31's basis restated, the interpolation caveat on 33 (R9), and the 11.4 dB called a stipulation (R13).

The eight short-only excisions are a different population and the text does not say so. They clear the gate by 0.1 to 1.7 scatters, on one class and one polarisation on six of the eight, with unresolved credits (rho_noise 0.2 to 0.6), on baselines the forecast of record does not price (R3). Within that population:

- 19: not excisable under the rule as written (R1); also on the persistence fallback (R2). Undetermined at the floor.
- 23 and 27: excised only by the persistence fallback; at their own finite probes both are inside or below the allowance (23: -3.0 to +1.9 dB; 27: -2.5 to +0.8 dB). Undetermined. 27 is also a switched-off channel with its long range 3.9 dB under tolerance at the floor (R10).
- 16: marginal on its own probes (2.8 to 4.6 dB), flipped by the credit ceiling and by the cleaner polarisation, both conceded in the text. Marginal, undetermined (R4).
- 25 and 26: on the persistence fallback, but they survive at every finite probe (6.5 to 9.8 and 4.8 to 11.4 dB). Excisable under stated assumptions (R2 option b or c); 26 is switched off and its source unidentified (R10).
- 15 and 22: measured coherence times (2097 and 2068 s, both grid interpolations worth 2.3 to 2.4 dB, R9), bars of 19.8 and 16.2 dB, small credits (0.5 and 1.2 dB) so the ceiling stipulation leaves 10 dB or more; at rho 0.99 15 sits at 3.3 dB and 22 at 0.4 (R7). The most defensible of the eight, subject to R3 and the single-polarisation disclosure (R5).

The framing therefore is not supported as stated. "Thirteen channels are excised" holds only if the code's departures from the written rule on 19, the persistence fallback on 23 and 27, the 0.95 ceiling and the 20 m baseline cut are all decided in the excising direction and stated as decisions; each one taken as the rule's own direction requires ("excise only with every credit the data can give", "a bound can excise but never keep") moves rows toward undetermined. The count the documents support today is five excised on the long BAO baselines with high confidence; four (15, 22, 25, 26) measured above the floor on the 9.8 m north-south class and over tolerance there under stipulated credits and, for 25 and 26, a stipulated persistence, on baselines the forecast has not priced; 16 marginal; and 19, 23, 27 undetermined. The confident message to the collaboration is the one the first audit gave and the table has since half-obscured: five channels are shown contaminated on the BAO baselines after every credit, and the short north-south classes show a real, marginal, single-polarisation excess on a further population that the present floor, credits and forecast cannot price. If the author wants the eight, the route is R3(a) (rebuild the bank on the as-built density), R2(b) (least-probe bars), R7 (per-polarisation rho, a measured ceiling) and R11 (uncertainties), in that order, and the abstract must then say that the short-only rows are single-class, single-polarisation detections at the floor's edge whose bars depend on those choices.
