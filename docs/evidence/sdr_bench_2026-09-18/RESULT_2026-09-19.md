# Single-input SDR bench, result, 2026-09-19

Session of 2026-09-18 and 19, LimeSDR Mini 1D423D9108F273, conducted, two 30 dB
pads, one cable, two terminators. Helper and worker unchanged; the worker binary
is the 2026-09-09 artifact, re-receipted as build-v2.json (only a test file had
changed, by commit 864e1c2, and the binary is byte-identical).

## The commanded-amplitude ladder failed, and that is a finding

The protocol as designed stepped the commanded tone amplitude. Measured on one
wiring, back to back, unsaturated, with the path verified to reproduce to 0.2 dB:

| commanded | expected | measured |
|---|---|---|
| 0.005 | 0 dB | 0 dB |
| 0.0025 | -6.02 | -18.0 |
| 0.00125 | -12.04 | -19.0 |
| 0.0003125 | -24.08 | -44.9 |
| 0.00015625 | -30.10 | -44.0 |

The response is a quantization floor in the transmit path, not a level control:
the first halving costs 18 dB, the second costs 1 dB, and the lowest amplitude
reads higher than the one above it. Commanded amplitude is therefore unusable as
a level knob on this device below 0.005, which is the value the 2026-09-09
records happened to use. The six rung protocols in rungs/ are void and are kept
only as the record of what was predeclared.

## The transmit-gain ladder passes

Amendment 2 fixed the amplitude at 0.005 and stepped the native transmit gain
over 50, 44, 38, 32, 26 with gain-50 brackets, in one wiring (one pad), 2 s per
record, requested and expected gain readbacks equal. Two independent passes:

| pass | qualified points | slope, dB per dB | residual scatter | bracket |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 5 (opening bracket void, path open) | 0.946 | 0.21 dB | not evaluable |
| B | 6 | 0.941 | 0.19 dB | -0.27 dB |

Predeclared criteria: slope 1.00 within 0.10, scatter at or below 1.0 dB,
bracket within 0.5 dB. Pass B meets all three; pass A agrees with it.

The 6 percent shortfall in slope is either the device's gain table or the
estimator, and this bench cannot separate them: both would appear as a slope
below one. Separating them needs a level step known independently of the radio,
which the pads could supply once their value is calibrated.

## Controls

- Terminated floor, target bin: 4.16e-4 to 4.32e-4 across five records spanning
  the session, so the floor is stable to 0.2 dB.
- Cabled floor with the transmitter disabled: 4.23e-4, within 0.1 dB of the
  terminated floor. No ambient contamination of the target bin, despite an urban
  site.
- Transmit chain enabled with zero IQ against transmit disabled: +0.09 dB in the
  target bin. The transmit chain does not raise the floor.
- The two 30 dB pads are equal within 0.2 dB. One clean pad step, unsaturated at
  both ends, measures 26.8 dB; the pads' absolute value is not established.
- The RF path reproduced to 0.2 dB across an hour and many reconnections.

## Transport

The radio reached WSL over a usbipd attachment made for this session, which is
not the path of the 2026-09-09 records. Every record carried dropped RX samples
in its startup phase, and three records had drops inside the measurement window;
the worker refused all three and they were re-recorded under amendment 1. No
record with a qualified-window drop entered the analysis. A quieter transport is
wanted before any longer session.

## What this closes

The single-input physical reference: a terminated noise floor and a tone stepped
by a known analog level through one input, measured by the unchanged estimator,
tracking with slope 0.94 and 0.19 dB scatter over 24 dB. It establishes nothing
about sensitivity, receiver operating characteristics, absolute power, array
degrees of freedom or the CHIME deployment, and it does not rehabilitate the
amplitude ladder.

## Note for a future session, 2026-09-19 (author)

Radiated emission is not a constraint at this site. The conducted fixture used
here was chosen by the protocol's own declaration, not by a limit imposed from
outside, so a future session may use the antennas if there is a reason to. There
is not one for this result: an over-the-air path adds an unknown, drifting link
loss and would weaken the ladder rather than strengthen it. What would settle the
one open question, whether the 6 percent slope shortfall belongs to the device
gain table or to the estimator, is a level step known independently of the radio:
attenuators with a calibration, or a power meter at the RX port. Neither is an
antenna.
